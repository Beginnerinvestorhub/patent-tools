# Design Patent Drawings (37 CFR 1.152 / MPEP § 1503.02)

Design patent drawings define the claim. They must fully disclose the ornamental appearance of the article. Formal requirements of 37 CFR 1.84 still apply (sheet size, margins, black lines, numeral height, etc.), with the additions and differences below.

## Core requirements
- Sufficient number of views to constitute a complete disclosure of the appearance of the design (typically front, rear, top, bottom, left side, right side, and one or more perspective views).
- Identical or flat views may be omitted if the specification states that the omitted view is identical or a mirror image of a shown view, or that the surface is flat and unornamented.
- Appropriate and adequate surface shading is required to show the character and contour of the surfaces and to distinguish open areas from solid areas.
- Solid black surface shading is permitted only when used to represent the color black or to show color contrast.
- Broken lines may be used to show visible environmental structure or to define the bounds of the claimed design, but may **not** be used to show hidden planes or surfaces that cannot be seen through opaque materials.
- Alternate positions of a design component illustrated by full and broken lines in the same view are not permitted.
- Photographs and ink drawings may not be mixed as formal drawings in one application.
- Photographs submitted in lieu of ink drawings must be limited to the claimed design and must not disclose environmental structure.

## Color in design applications
- Color drawings or photographs are permitted without a petition.
- The specification must contain the required statement referring to the color drawings (similar language to the utility color petition statement).
- Using color narrows claim scope to the specific color scheme shown.

## Surface shading guidance
- Use light stippling, line shading, or other conventional techniques that remain clear after reduction and reproduction.
- Avoid dense solid fills except for the limited solid-black cases noted above.
- Shading must not obscure the outline of the design or any reference characters (if used).

## Reference characters and views
- View numbers and reference characters are less critical than in utility applications but should still follow 37 CFR 1.84 conventions when used.
- Every claimed ornamental feature must be clearly visible in at least one view.
- The drawings must satisfy 35 U.S.C. § 112 enablement and definiteness — nothing about the claimed design may be left to conjecture.

## Common objections specific to design drawings
- Insufficient views leaving the appearance incomplete.
- Missing or inadequate surface shading that fails to convey contour.
- Improper use of broken lines (especially for hidden structure).
- Mixing photographs with line drawings as formal figures.
- Environmental structure shown in solid lines when it should be broken.

When the invention is primarily ornamental appearance rather than function, switch to these design-specific rules. Flag to the user that claim scope is defined by the drawings themselves.
