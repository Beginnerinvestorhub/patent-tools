# Plant Patent Drawings (37 CFR 1.165 / MPEP § 1606)

Plant patent drawings must disclose all the distinctive characteristics of the plant that are capable of visual representation. They must comply with the formal standards of 37 CFR 1.84.

## Key differences from utility drawings
- View numbers and reference characters need not be employed unless required by the examiner.
- The drawings must be artistically and competently executed.
- Color drawings or photographs are permitted and are **required** if color is a distinguishing characteristic of the new variety.
- **No petition is needed** for color in a plant application (37 CFR 1.165(b)). The 37 CFR 1.84(a)(2) color petition applies to utility applications only.
- When color is used, 37 CFR 1.165(b) calls for two copies of each color drawing or photograph. How Patent Center handles the second copy for electronic filings is a procedural point to confirm with current USPTO guidance or the user's attorney; say so rather than guessing.
- If the required color copies are missing, the application still receives a filing date, but correction is required before examination proceeds.

## Content expectations
- Show all distinctive visual characteristics (habit, flower form and color, foliage, fruit, etc.).
- Scale and detail must be adequate so that the distinguishing features can be clearly seen.
- Photographs are commonly accepted when they accurately depict the plant characteristics.

## Practical notes
- Watercolor renderings and high-quality photographs are both used.
- Identifying indicia (title, inventor, application/docket number) may still be placed in the top margin.
- The same sheet-size, margin, and line-quality rules of 37 CFR 1.84 apply to any line-drawing elements.

Treat plant drawings as a specialized subset. Always ask whether color is a distinguishing trait; if it is, prepare and submit the required duplicate color sets.
