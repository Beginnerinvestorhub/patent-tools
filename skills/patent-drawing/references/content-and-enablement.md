# Content, Enablement, and Claim Support Rules (37 CFR 1.83 and related)

Formatting compliance is necessary but not sufficient. The drawings must also satisfy substantive disclosure rules.

## 37 CFR 1.83 — Content of the drawing
- The drawing in a nonprovisional application **must show every feature of the invention specified in the claims**.
- Conventional features disclosed in the description and claims, where detailed illustration is not essential, should be shown as a graphical drawing symbol or a labeled representation (e.g., a labeled rectangular box).
- Tables included in the specification and sequences included in sequence listings should **not** be duplicated in the drawings.
- When the invention is an improvement on an old machine, the drawing should, when possible:
  - Exhibit the improved portion itself, disconnected from the old structure, and
  - In another view, show only so much of the old structure as is necessary to illustrate the connection of the invention therewith.

## When drawings are required
- 35 U.S.C. § 113 / 37 CFR 1.81: Furnish a drawing where necessary for the understanding of the subject matter sought to be patented.
- For applications filed on or after 18 December 2013 (other than design applications), a drawing is **not** required to obtain a filing date. However, drawings should still be filed promptly if needed, because post-filing additions cannot introduce new matter.
- Design applications always require drawings for a filing date.
- The examiner may require submission of drawings (or additional views) within a period of not less than two months if the subject matter admits of illustration.
- Drawings submitted after the filing date may not be used to overcome an insufficiency of the specification or to supplement the original disclosure for claim interpretation.

## Software / computer-implemented inventions
- Flowcharts should mirror the steps of the method claims (especially the broadest claim).
- Block diagrams should show the significant system components and their relationships.
- Recommended practical set for most software applications:
  1. Network or operating environment.
  2. Main computing node / server / device.
  3. High-level method flowchart corresponding to the broadest claim.
  4. One or more detailed or alternative flowcharts.
  5. System diagram showing the computer-readable medium / memory / processor relationship.
- UI/GUI figures are appropriate when the visual appearance or specific interaction is claimed or needed for enablement.
- Keep labels inside flowchart boxes short; put explanatory detail in the written description.

## Cross-checks that prevent common rejections
- Every reference numeral appearing in the drawings must appear in the written description (and vice versa for drawn elements).
- The Brief Description of the Drawings section in the specification must accurately describe each figure.
- Claimed features must be visible and identifiable in the figures.
- Consistency of terminology and numbering between claims, description, and drawings is essential.

Failure on these content points often produces enablement, written-description, or indefiniteness rejections under 35 U.S.C. § 112, or drawing objections under 37 CFR 1.83, even when the formal 1.84 rules are satisfied.
